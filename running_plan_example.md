## Block Success and Progression Criteria

The block succeeds if:

- at least six of eight weeks contain four runs without cramming missed mileage;
- two or more weeks reach 27-31 mi with normal next-day recovery;
- the athlete completes a controlled 13-14 mi long run;
- tempo work progresses from 18 to 25-30 total minutes without late-repetition fade;
- the standard aerobic route shows stable or improved pace/HR under comparable conditions;
- calf tightness remains mild, transient, and non-progressive.

Progress only when:

- the preceding week was completed with normal mechanics;
- sleep, fatigue, and symptoms do not show a negative cluster;
- easy effort remains controlled;
- the athlete can absorb the current load rather than merely finish it.

Hold or recover when those conditions are not met. The absence of a race is not a reason to avoid progression when they are met.

## Training Intensity Rules

Easy runs:

- Mostly 135-150 bpm and RPE 3-4.
- Begin gently; do not chase pace in the first two miles.
- Slow down for heat, hills, fatigue, or poor sleep.

Long runs:

- Mostly 138-153 bpm and RPE 3-5.
- Brief HR rises on hills are acceptable; sustained hard breathing is not.
- Distance is conditional on normal mechanics and stable symptoms.

Controlled tempo:

- RPE 6-7: purposeful but repeatable.
- Finish able to complete one more repetition with good form.
- Convert to easy running when recovery or warmup response is abnormal.

## Current 7-Day Adjustment

- Keep the planned structure if sleep, resting HR, HRV trend, and calf symptoms remain normal.
- If one recovery signal is poor, keep the run easy and reduce it by 10-20%.
- If two or more signals are poor, remove the workout and consider rest.
- Do not move missed quality work onto Friday or into the long run.

## Workout Menu

Strides:

- 4-6 x 15-20 seconds relaxed-fast after an easy run
- Full easy recovery; stop before form deteriorates

Short hills:

- 6-8 x 30-45 seconds at controlled strong effort
- Avoid while calf or Achilles symptoms are active

Tempo intervals:

- 2-4 repetitions of 5-10 minutes at RPE 6-7
- Use easy jogging recoveries and preserve even execution

## Fueling Practice

- Begin on runs longer than about 75 minutes.
- Mock starting target: 30-45 g carbohydrate per hour.
- Record product, amount, timing, fluid, weather, and gastrointestinal response.
- Increase only when the current dose is well tolerated.

## Strength and Cross-Training

- Two short sessions per week when they do not impair key runs
- Priorities: calf/soleus strength, single-leg control, hips, hamstrings, and trunk
- Keep new strength work conservative near long runs
- Easy cycling or walking can support recovery but does not replace run-specific durability

## Adjustment Rules

Poor sleep:

- One poor night: easy running may remain appropriate if warmup and symptoms are normal.
- Multiple poor nights: remove intensity and reduce volume.

Soreness or pain:

- Diffuse mild soreness that improves during warmup: shorten and keep easy.
- Pain that changes stride, becomes sharper, or worsens: stop.

Illness:

- Fever, chest symptoms, or meaningful systemic illness: no running; resume conservatively after recovery.

Missed runs:

- Do not cram mileage into later days.
- Preserve recovery spacing and prioritize the next appropriate session.

Abnormal HR response:

- Slow down and compare with breathing, heat, terrain, hydration, and sensor quality.
- End early when HR remains unusually high alongside poor effort or symptoms.
